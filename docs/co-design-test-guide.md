# Co-design test guide

For LUMA team members running short sessions with students who have albinism or low vision. Each session takes about 40 minutes with one student. The aim is to find what is hard, not to prove LUMA works. Record what the student does and says, then decide what to change.

## Before the session

- **Consent**: explain the session in the student's preferred language. Get written consent (and a guardian's, if the student is under 18) for notes and, only if agreed, audio. No photos of the student.
- **Phone**: the pilot APK installed, battery above 60%, TalkBack off unless the student uses it, Android font size at the student's usual setting. Delete earlier test readings (Privacy and about > Delete all my readings).
- **Material**: a real class handout the student has **not** seen, printed on ordinary paper. Use a page with a date, a name and numbers. If you can, also use a photo of a board.
- **Online part**: check `/v1/capabilities` shows the student's language as available. Have mobile data or Wi-Fi ready to switch on.
- **Two people**: one facilitator speaks; one note-taker writes. Agree that neither will touch the phone unless asked.

## Task 1: read an unfamiliar page (15 minutes)

Say: *"Here is a handout from a lesson. Please use LUMA to read it the way you like. Tell me what you are thinking as you go."*

Watch, without helping:

1. Setting up the Vision Profile: which size, spacing and colours did they choose? Did they skip?
2. Scanning: was the camera easy to frame? Did a retake instruction appear, and did it help?
3. Reading: any sideways dragging, lost place, words hard to read, buttons hard to find or hit?
4. The photo: did they open it? Could they zoom?
5. Saving: can they find the reading again from the home screen?

Then ask: *"What is the date on this page?"* and *"Who is the teacher?"* Note whether the answer is correct and how they found it.

## Task 2: an unrehearsed voice question (10 minutes)

Turn on the connection. Say: *"Ask LUMA anything you want to know about this page, by speaking, in the language you prefer."* Do not suggest a question.

Note:

1. Did they understand "What leaves your phone"?
2. Recording: start, stop, send. Any confusion?
3. Transcript: was it right? Did they correct it, especially names and mixed-language words?
4. Answer: in their language? Correct against the page? Did they tap a cited line to check?
5. Hearing the answer: did they find Hear answer and Stop? Was the voice understandable?

If something fails (no connection, unavailable language), note what the student did next and whether the message helped.

## Close (5 minutes)

- *"What was hardest?"*
- *"What would you change first?"*
- *"Would you use this in class? When?"*

## Record of findings

Fill in one block per session. Leave it blank until a session has happened. Do not paraphrase into what the team hoped to hear.
